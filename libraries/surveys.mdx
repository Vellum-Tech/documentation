A survey template is governed wording for collecting evidence from
beneficiaries: the questions, the timing, and, first class on every template,
the approved consent text shown before anyone answers. When an outcome lacks
data, a curated template is how the gap becomes evidence the engine can use.

## The survey list

Open **Libraries → Survey library**.

![The survey library list with one curated template showing timing, question count and consent](/images/libraries/surveys-list.jpg)

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Title | The template's name |
| Outcome | The outcome the survey evidences |
| Segment | The beneficiary segment the survey addresses |
| Timing | Pre, Post or Follow-up, relative to the intervention |
| Questions | How many questions the template holds |
| Consent | Whether approved consent text is set |
| Status | Draft, Curated or Archived |

Search by title, outcome or segment, and filter by **Timing** or **Status**.
A template without consent text reads **No consent text** in the list: that
is an unmet obligation, not a blank. Bulk archive is available from the
list's checkboxes.

## The survey record

Click a row to open the template.

![A survey template record with consent and privacy, details and questions cards](/images/libraries/survey-record.jpg)

- **Consent & privacy** leads the record: the approved consent wording shown
  before a beneficiary answers, and the privacy rule describing how responses
  may be used and aggregated. A banner calls out a missing consent text.
- **Survey details**: title, outcome hint, beneficiary segment and timing.
- **Questions**: the ordered prompts, each with a kind: **Scale**, **Text**
  or **Yes / No**.
- **Notes**: context or caveats.

### The consent gate

The status pill next to the title promotes a template from **Draft** to
**Curated**, and the promotion is gated on consent: a template without
consent text cannot be curated, and the menu says why (**Add consent text
first**) rather than letting the attempt fail. A curated template therefore
always carries approved consent wording.

## Create a template

<Note>
Creating and editing survey templates is staff only. The **New template**
button does not appear for client organisation members.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the dialog">
    In **Libraries → Survey library**, click **New template**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the survey">
    Enter the title and, optionally, the outcome it evidences, the
    beneficiary segment it addresses, and its timing (**Pre**, **Post** or
    **Follow-up**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the questions">
    Add each prompt and pick its kind: **Scale**, **Text** or **Yes / No**.
    Use **Add question** for more rows; empty prompts are dropped on save.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set consent and privacy">
    Write the consent text a beneficiary sees before answering, and the
    privacy rule for how responses are used and aggregated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it">
    Click **Add template**. It lands as a draft; once the consent text is in
    place you can promote it to **Curated** from its record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How templates relate to gaps and evidence

The template's outcome hint names the outcome it evidences, and its timing
says when it is put to beneficiaries relative to the intervention. When a
project's outcome has no data yet, the governed template gives the project
approved wording, with consent built in, for gathering that evidence, so the
numbers the engine later computes rest on properly collected responses.
