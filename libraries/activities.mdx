An **activity** is something an organisation does: food rescue and redistribution, one-to-one mentoring, home energy retrofits, respite weekends. The Activity library is your firm's agreed catalogue of those phrases.

It holds words, not numbers. An activity entry never carries a value, a cost or a rate. Those come later, from the client's data and from your [factors](/libraries/factors).

## Why it exists

Because the same programme gets described five different ways by five different consultants, and by the time it reaches a report nobody can tell whether "food redistribution", "food rescue" and "surplus food programme" are one activity or three.

A governed catalogue fixes that. Agree the phrase once, tidy it, mark it **Curated**, and everyone building an impact model reaches for the same wording. Reports become comparable across clients and across years, which is exactly what a portfolio-level view needs.

The library also carries useful context that saves people time: which impact domain an activity sits in, which SDGs it usually relates to, and hints about who typically benefits and what typically changes.

## The activity list

Open **Libraries > Activity library**.

Each row shows:

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Activity** | The phrase itself. |
| **Domain** | The impact domain it belongs to. |
| **SDGs** | The Sustainable Development Goals it's tagged with. |
| **Observed** | How often this phrase recurred in the source material the library was built from. It's a popularity signal used to rank suggestions, nothing more. It is not a count of projects and carries no weight in any calculation. |
| **Status** | **Draft**, **Curated** or **Archived**. |

Search matches the activity phrase and its domain name. The **Domain** and **Status** filter chips narrow the list, and **View options > Grouping** lets you cluster by either.

### The impact domains

Six governed domains, the same everywhere in Vellum:

- Health, disability and medical research
- Education, training and employment
- Climate, energy and carbon
- Built environment and housing
- Circular economy and waste
- Agriculture, food and nature

Setting a domain is optional, but it's what lets Vellum narrow down the valuation approaches likely to apply once an activity is in play, so it's worth doing.

## The activity record

Click a row to open it. The phrase is the heading, with a status pill beside it. Click the pill to change status. It saves immediately.

**Activity details** holds the **Activity** phrase, its **Impact domain**, its **SDGs** and a **Description** of what the activity actually involves.

**Authoring hints** holds an **Example beneficiary** and an **Example outcome**: the group that typically benefits and the change that typically follows. These are suggestions offered to whoever is building an impact model. They're never applied automatically. Someone always chooses.

**Notes** is free text for context, caveats, or a note on how the phrase is worded and why.

At the foot of the record is a quiet line showing when the activity was added and by whom.

## How to add an activity

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new activity">
    Open **Libraries > Activity library** and click **New activity**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the phrase">
    Type the **Activity** phrase. Aim for something a client would recognise: "Food rescue and redistribution", not "FR programme".
  </Step>
  <Step title="File it">
    Optionally set the **Impact domain** and tag any **SDGs**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it">
    Click **Add activity**. Vellum opens the new record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

New activities land as **Draft**. That's intentional: adding to a shared library shouldn't require a committee, but promoting something to the firm's agreed vocabulary should be a deliberate act.

## How to edit an activity

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the card">
    Open the activity and find the card you want to change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Edit">
    Click **Edit** on that card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make your changes">
    Change whatever you need.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**, or **Cancel** to discard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Changes appear straight away. If a save fails, Vellum puts the old value back and tells you. It never silently drops an edit.

## How to curate an activity

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the entry">
    Open the activity and check the wording, domain, SDGs and description are right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the status pill">
    Click the status pill beside the heading.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Curated">
    Choose **Curated**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Curated** means "this is the firm's agreed phrase". **Draft** means "someone added this in the flow of work and it hasn't been reviewed". Both are usable. The status just tells a colleague which is which.

## How to archive activities

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the rows">
    In the list, tick the rows you want to archive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Archive">
    Click **Archive** on the bar that appears at the bottom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Archiving is soft and reversible. Archived entries stay in the library marked **Archived**, and can be restored by opening the record and setting the status back.

## How activities are used

When someone builds a project's impact model, each row pairs an activity with a beneficiary and an outcome. The activity picker offers this library, and if they type a phrase that isn't in it, they're offered a tick box, *Add this activity to the Activity Library so it can be reused*, which is ticked by default.

That's how the library is meant to grow: from real work, not from a workshop. Which also means it needs occasional tidying, because it will accumulate near-duplicates.

## Tips and gotchas

**Write the phrase, not the project name.** "Mentoring sessions for young people" travels between clients. "SPARK Phase 2" doesn't.

**Check before you add.** Search first. Two entries meaning the same thing is the exact problem this library exists to prevent.

**Curate deliberately, and in batches.** A useful rhythm is to filter to **Draft**, work down the list tidying wording and setting domains, and promote as you go.

**Observed is a hint, not evidence.** It ranks suggestions. It's not a project count and it never enters a calculation.

**Authoring hints are suggestions.** They appear as prompts to whoever is building a model. Nothing is applied automatically, so a hint that's usually right but occasionally wrong is still worth recording.

**Archive rather than delete.** Old reports referenced these phrases. Archiving keeps the history intact while getting the entry out of everyone's way.
