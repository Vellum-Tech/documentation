The policy library is the firm-wide list of public policy a project's work
can align with: acts, instruments, policies, strategies and grant programmes.
An entry records the policy's public facts only, its words, codes and dates,
never a figure, and every active entry keeps an archived copy of its official
source so a report's citation still opens years later.

## The policy list

Open **Libraries → Policy library**. The list is grouped by level by default.

![The policy library grouped by level with four active entries and one draft](/images/libraries/policies-list.jpg)

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Policy | The title, with the issuer beneath |
| Kind | Act, Instrument, Policy, Strategy or Grant programme |
| Applies to | The jurisdiction the policy applies to |
| Theme | The policy's theme, such as Climate and energy |
| Archived copy | Whether a copy of the source is kept |
| Status | Draft, Active or Retired |

Search by title, issuer or register id, and filter by **Kind** or **Status**.
Every entry is listed whatever its status, so a curator can find a draft to
activate.

## The policy record

Click an entry to open it.

![A policy record showing the policy facts and its archived copy](/images/libraries/policy-record.jpg)

- **Policy**: the issuer, what it does, the official page, kind, the level
  and jurisdiction it applies to, theme, register id, the dates it is in
  force, and the SDGs the policy itself targets. For a grant programme, the
  record also shows what it gives (cash, land, a tax concession or in kind)
  and its opening and closing dates.
- **Archived copy**: the kept copy of the current version's source, with how
  it was kept (fetched from the official page, or uploaded by a person), the
  address it came from, the file type and size, and its SHA-256 fingerprint.
  A report's footnote opens this copy, so an entry is activated only once it
  has one.
- **Versions**: every version of the entry's words, newest first. Superseded
  versions stay readable because a report's footnote points at the version it
  read.

## The lifecycle: propose, activate, retire

Anyone can propose a policy. It lands as a draft, and only a library curator
can activate or retire it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Propose">
    In **Libraries → Policy library**, click **Propose policy** and describe
    the public policy: its title, issuer, what it does, the official page,
    kind, theme, level and jurisdiction, dates, source language, the register
    that lists it and its register id, and the goals the policy itself
    targets. Why a project aligns with it is written on the project's link,
    never here.

    ![The propose a policy dialog filled in for a sample grant programme](/images/libraries/policy-propose-dialog.jpg)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draft">
    Click **Propose**. The entry lands as a **Draft** at version 1 with no
    archived copy yet. A draft can be linked in the project it was proposed
    in, but a curator must archive its source and activate it before any
    other project can use it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep a copy">
    On the entry's record, keep a copy of the official page. Vellum can fetch
    it, or, where an issuer refuses automated fetches, you can save the page
    or PDF yourself and upload it; an uploaded copy is as good as a fetched
    one. Whoever proposed a draft may keep its copy; once an entry is active,
    keeping a new version's copy is a curator's job.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activate">
    A curator clicks **Activate** on the record. Activation requires the
    archived copy, because reports cite it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retire, eventually">
    When a policy lapses or is repealed, a curator clicks **Retire**. The
    entry, its versions and its archived copies remain readable; existing
    report citations keep working.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Editing an active entry

Editing an entry's words appends a new version rather than changing the old
one. On a draft the new version is current at once; on an active entry it is
pending until a curator keeps its own archived copy, and until then every
report keeps citing the previous version.

## Who curators are

Library curator is a per-person grant, separate from the admin role. An admin
turns it on or off with the **Library curator** switch beside each person's
role on the Team page; see [Team](/admin/team). Curators may activate and
retire policy entries and edit active entries' words. Everything else about
the library, including proposing drafts, needs no special grant.

<Note>
If you act without the curator grant, the action is refused with a message
saying a curator is required; nothing is hidden behind silently disabled
buttons.
</Note>

## How policies connect to projects

A project links the policies its work aligns with, and writes the reason for
each alignment on the project's link. The Workbench's policy alignment sheet
is where links are made and maintained, and the report's policy block renders
them with footnotes that open the archived source copies. See
[Workbench](/projects/workbench) and
[Report builder](/projects/report-builder).
