---
title: "The Workbench"
description: "The project worksheet where every impact value is modelled, explained and totalled."
---

The Workbench is the project's chart of impact accounts: one worksheet with a
row for each combination of an activity, who benefits from it, and the outcome
they get. Every dollar on the sheet is computed by Vellum's calculation engine
from the evidence behind it. Nobody types a dollar figure into the Workbench,
and the AI assistant never produces one; you change a value by changing the
data it is built from, and the engine recomputes.

![The Workbench worksheet with two rows, one for each activity, showing the activity, beneficiary and outcome columns](/images/workbench/worksheet.jpg)

## Reading a row

A row reads left to right as a sentence: the activity, what went into it, who
benefits and by how much, the outcome, how that outcome is valued, and the
value the engine computed. The main columns are:

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Activity** | The program or service, with its description and recorded inputs beside it. |
| **Activity input** and **Activity output value** | The money and resources in, and what they produced. Both merge across the activity's rows. |
| **Beneficiary** and **Beneficiary %** | Who benefits, and their share of the activity. |
| **Outcome** and **Outcome %** | The change they experience, and its weighting. |
| **Valuation method** | The method valuing the outcome, with its version. |
| **Valued from** | Whether the row is valued from outcome data or from a share of the activity's direct cost. |
| **Outcome formula** | The recipe in words, with each data point named: for example Saving (D-02 · 250) × Households (D-01 · 100). Numbers never hide in this cell; it names where they come from. |
| **SDGs** and **SDG %** | The Sustainable Development Goals the outcome serves, and the split between them. |
| **Status** | The row's evidence status (see below). |
| **Outcome value** | The engine's computed value for the row. |
| **Policies** | Small count columns beside the activity, beneficiary, outcome and SDG families, showing how many policy links name that subject. |
| **Comments** | The row's conversation state. |

![The right half of the worksheet showing the valuation method, formula, SDGs, status and outcome value columns](/images/workbench/worksheet-right.jpg)

Hover over an **Outcome value** cell and a small lock appears: the figure is
the engine's own output and never takes a typed value. Clicking it shows how
the value was computed and how it is attributed across goals.

The **Status** column is a pick, chosen on each row:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Measured** | The value rests on measured data. |
| **Estimated** | The value rests on an estimate. |
| **Proxy** | A stand-in measure is used. |
| **Placeholder** | A temporary figure to be replaced. |
| **Future survey needed** | Evidence is planned but not collected yet. |

A thin bar on each row's left edge carries the row's working state: amber for
waiting on the client, green for a new response to review. Click **Key** at
the top left of the sheet for the legend.

<Tip>
Use the **Columns** button to show or hide any column (the ID column is hidden
by default), and drag a column header to reorder. **Show all** and **Reset to
defaults** live in the same popover.
</Tip>

## Opening a row's calculation

Click a row's **Outcome formula** cell to open the calculation drawer for that
activity. The first screen shows the activity's chain: **Input**, **Output**
and **Value**, each the engine's figure, plus where the value comes from and
which reports use it.

![The calculation drawer showing the input, output and value chain for an activity and the reports that use it](/images/workbench/calculation-chain.jpg)

Drill into the outcome's line and you see the full working:

![The outcome line screen with the valuation method, the operands it needs and the computed result](/images/workbench/calculation-line.jpg)

- **Valuation method** names the formula and its version (for example v1).
  **Edit method** changes it, and **Open in Library** shows it in the
  [valuation methodology library](/libraries/valuation-methodologies).
- **What it needs** lists the formula's operands, one row each, with the data
  point feeding it: a registry row such as D-01 · Households audited · 100.
  Each operand is bound to real data; you can pick a data point the project
  holds, pull one from a document in the [data room](/projects/data-room), or
  type a new value, which is recorded as a registry row of its own rather than
  disappearing into the cell.
- The footer shows the formula with its operands and the engine's result, and
  **Update line** rewrites this outcome's own line.

Nothing in the drawer does arithmetic in your browser. It sends the bindings
to the engine and shows the engine's answer.

## Comments on a row

The **Comments** column carries each row's conversation state, such as a
pending question or an unread reply, and opens the row's comment panel, where
questions to the client are asked and answered beside the row they concern.
On workspaces where the comment stream is not available yet, the panel says
so; the column stays quiet until it arrives.

## The totals panel

Click the **Total impact** figure in the sheet header to open the totals
panel. It shows the project total and the same figure rolled up three ways:
**By SDG**, **By outcome** and **By beneficiary**, each with a **Chart** and
**Table** view. Every bar, dollar and share is served by the engine; the
panel sums nothing itself. It stays open while you edit weightings, so the
totals move beside the cells that move them.

![The totals panel showing total impact broken down by SDG, by outcome and by beneficiary](/images/workbench/totals-panel.jpg)

## Open gaps

Beside the header buttons sits the open-gap count, for example **3 open
gaps**, or **No open gaps** when the register is clear. It links to the Gaps
sheet in the project's [data room](/projects/data-room), where missing data is
recorded and resolved. Open gaps are left out of the totals by design, so the
sheet never carries a number that is still being chased.

## The Policy alignment sub-sheet

Click **Policy alignment** in the sheet header to swap the worksheet for the
policy sheet (click it again to go back). It records how the project's work
aligns with government and international policy: one row per link between a
subject on the Workbench and a policy from the
[policy library](/libraries/policies).

![The policy alignment sheet with five links, each naming a subject, a policy, a provision, a claim and a rationale](/images/workbench/policy-sheet.jpg)

| Column | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject** | An activity, beneficiary, outcome or SDG from this project. |
| **Policy** | The policy the subject aligns with. |
| **Provision** | The section or priority within the policy, as text. |
| **Claim** | **Aligns with** or **Supported by**. |
| **Quantity** | An optional measurable the policy names. |
| **Rationale** | One or two sentences of why, in your words. |
| **Proposed by** | Analyst, or the agent for pending proposals. |
| **Approved** | The approval date, or the **Approve** and **Dismiss** actions on a pending proposal. |

To add a link:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Add a link">
    A blank row lands at the bottom of the sheet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill the row">
    **Subject**, **Policy** and **Claim** are typed lookups; **Provision** and
    **Rationale** are text. The row saves as soon as it has a subject, a
    policy, a claim and a rationale.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Two kinds of wording are refused on save, with the reason shown on the row and
your text kept for editing: a rationale that quotes a figure (a policy's words
never carry a number; figures come from the engine), and compliance wording
such as "complies with" (alignment is your assessment, never a finding of
compliance). Duplicates of an existing link are refused too. A saved row's
subject and policy are its identity; remove the row and add a new one to
change them.

**Suggest links** asks the assistant to propose links in chat; proposals land
on the sheet as pending rows for a person to **Approve** or **Dismiss**. The
**Dismissed** toggle lists dismissals, each with **Restore**. The per-subject
**Policies** counts on the worksheet open this sheet filtered to that subject.

## Exporting the Workbench

The **Actions** menu in the sheet header holds two exports:

- **Export .xlsx** downloads the whole model as an Excel workbook: a
  **Workbench** sheet mirroring the worksheet, a **Totals** sheet with the
  three rollups, **Registry** and **Formulas** reference sheets (plus
  bindings and comments sheets where the project has them), and a
  **Metadata** sheet recording when and what was exported. Where a row has a
  stored formula, the workbook places a live Excel translation of it beside
  the engine's figure, never instead of it, so the numbers can be checked in
  Excel without ever replacing them.
- **Send request** builds a small Data request workbook of the registry rows
  still asked of the client, with the columns ID, Parameter, Unit, Scope,
  What we need, Owner and Notes, ready to send on. It is available once at
  least one row is waiting on the client; until then it reads "nothing
  asked".
