A **factor** is a number your firm has sourced from somewhere reputable and is prepared to stand behind: the replacement cost of a meal, the wellbeing value of a volunteer hour, the public cost avoided when someone doesn't present at an emergency department.

Every factor has four things that matter: a **name**, a **unit**, a **value**, and a **source citation**. The first three make it usable. The fourth makes it defensible.

## Why it exists

<Note>
  Vellum will not let anyone type a dollar figure onto a report. Every number in a valuation comes either from the client's own measured data or from a factor in this library — which means the question "where did this number come from?" always has an answer, and that answer is a citation you can hand to a reviewer.
</Note>

The library also stops the same number being re-sourced, re-argued and slightly re-invented on every engagement. Source it once, cite it properly, and every project after that inherits your work.

There's one more reason, and it's the quiet one: **versioning**. A factor's value changes over time — prices move, statistics get revised. When that happens you add a new version rather than overwriting the old one. A report signed off last year still traces to the number that was true when you signed it, and you can show exactly when and why the value moved.

## The factor list

Open **Libraries → Factor library** and you'll see every factor your firm holds.

Each row leads with the factor's **Name**, with a small external-link icon if the source has a URL — click it to open the source in a new tab. Underneath sits a quiet context line: the factor's category (or its type if there's no category), its unit, and a note like "3 versions" when the factor has history.

The columns to the right are:

- **Value** — the current version's number.
- **Unit** — what the number is per: *per meal*, *per hour*, *per tonne CO2e*.
- **Source** — a coloured pill showing the source tier, from **Govt statistic** down to **Other**.
- **Locale** — where the factor applies, where that's recorded.
- **Used in** — how many projects currently apply this factor. A blank here means nobody's used it yet.
- **Year** — the period the value relates to.

Use the search box to find a factor by name, unit, locale or source tier. The **Source** filter chip narrows to particular tiers, and **View options** lets you group the list by source tier — a fast way to see how much of your library rests on government statistics versus internal assumption.

## The factor record

Click a row to open the factor. The title carries a status pill you can click to change status directly — **Draft**, **In review**, **Approved**, **Deprecated** or **Archived**. Picking a new status saves it straight away.

Below that are four cards.

**Factor details** holds the definition — **Name**, **Unit**, **Factor type**, **Impact domain**, **Category** and **Priority SDGs**. None of this is versioned; it's how the factor is described and filed, not what it asserts.

**Value & source** holds everything that *is* versioned: the **Current value**, its **Value type**, the **Version** number, the **Source tier**, **Review status**, the effective period, **Base year**, **Geography**, and the **Source** citation with its link. Editing this card records a new version — the button even says **Save new version**, so there's no way to change a value by accident.

**Methodology notes** is free text: how the factor was derived, what caveats apply, how often it should be refreshed. Worth filling in. It's the field that saves a colleague from misusing your factor two years from now.

**Version history** lists every version the factor has ever had — version number, effective period, value, who changed it, and the change note. The current version is marked **current**; superseded ones are dimmed but never deleted.

**Usage** shows which projects apply this factor, with the client, the activity, the version they pinned, and the value that was actually applied. If a factor turns out to be wrong, this card tells you immediately who is affected.

## Field reference

**Source tier** — how authoritative the source is, from most to least: **Govt statistic**, **Peer reviewed**, **Industry report**, **Firm internal**, **Other**. This is the single most useful signal about a factor's quality, and it's what the list groups and filters by.

**Factor type** — what kind of number this is: **Monetary proxy**, **Conversion factor**, **Activity input**, **Rate or allocation**, **Formula parameter**, **Benchmark** or **Assumption**.

**Impact domain** — **Environment**, **Social**, **Economic**, **Governance** or **Mixed**.

**Status** — where the factor sits in your firm's curation: **Draft**, **In review**, **Approved**, **Deprecated**, **Archived**. Use **Deprecated** for a factor you no longer want applied to new work but must keep visible because old reports depend on it.

**Review status** — where this particular *version* sits in review: **Candidate**, **Needs source**, **In review**, **Approved**, **Rejected**, **Superseded**. Status describes the factor; review status describes the version's evidence.

**Value type** — **Point value**, **Range (min–max)**, **Text** or **Formula**. The value box records a single number, so **Point value** is what you'll use when adding factors by hand; the other types appear on factors that arrived from an import and display their range or text in the version history.

**Currency** — AUD, USD, GBP, EUR, NZD, JPY or CNY. Note that currency lives here, not in the unit, so a unit stays a clean denominator like *per meal* rather than doubling up as "$ per meal".

**Base year** and **Period year** — the year the value is expressed in, and the year it describes. They're often the same; when they aren't, both matter for comparability.

**Geography** — where the value applies, as free text: "NSW, AU", "United Kingdom", "National".

## How to add a factor

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the library and start a new factor">
    Open **Libraries → Factor library** and click **New factor**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Under **Definition**, give it a **Name** that will read clearly to someone who didn't source it — "Social value of volunteering" rather than "vol_val_2". Add a **Description** if the name doesn't carry enough.
  </Step>
  <Step title="File it">
    Set the **Factor type**, **Impact domain**, **Category** and **Status**. All optional, all worth doing — they're what make the library searchable when it holds four hundred factors instead of forty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag the SDGs">
    Tag any **Priority SDGs** the factor relates to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the value and unit">
    Under **Current value**, enter the **Value** and pick the **Unit**. The unit picker searches your firm's existing units; if what you need isn't there, type it and choose the **Add** option to create it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the value">
    Set the **Value type**, **Currency**, **Base year**, **Period year** and **Geography** as they apply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cite the source">
    Under **Source**, enter the **Source citation** — this is required. Write it as you'd write it in a footnote: "HACT Social Value Bank 2022", not "HACT".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the source detail">
    Add the **Source URL** if there is one, then set the **Source tier** and **Review status**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save factor**. Vellum opens the new factor's record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Name**, **Unit**, **Value** and **Source citation** are all required. If you're missing the citation, you're not ready to add the factor.
</Note>

## How to edit a factor's details

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the details card">
    Open the factor and find the **Factor details** card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Edit">
    Click **Edit**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change what you need">
    Change the **Name**, **Unit**, **Factor type**, **Impact domain**, **Category** or **Priority SDGs**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

These edits don't create a version — you're changing how the factor is described, not what it claims. The same applies to **Methodology notes** and to the status pill.

## How to add a new version

Do this whenever the *value* or the *source* changes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the value card">
    Open the factor and find the **Value & source** card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Edit">
    Click **Edit**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the value and evidence">
    Update the **Value**, and update the **Source citation**, **Source URL**, **Source tier** or **Review status** if the evidence has moved too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the period and place">
    Adjust **Base year**, **Period year** and **Geography** so they describe the new value rather than the old one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a change note">
    Write a **Change note** — a sentence saying what changed and why. It's optional and you should write it anyway; it's what makes the version history readable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the new version">
    Click **Save new version**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The previous version is kept, marked as superseded, and stays visible in **Version history**. Projects that already pinned the old version keep it — check the **Usage** card to see who they are.

## Tips and gotchas

<Tip>
  **Write the citation for a stranger.** The person who reads it in three years won't be you, and they won't have your browser tabs open. Include the publisher, the title and the year.
</Tip>

**Source tier is a judgement, so make it honestly.** Marking a firm assumption as an industry report doesn't strengthen the report — it just moves the weakness somewhere harder to find. **Firm internal** and **Other** are legitimate tiers; the point of the ladder is that a reader can see where you stand.

<Warning>
  **Never edit a value to "fix" it.** Add a version. The old number is what old reports used, and pretending otherwise breaks the audit trail that makes Vellum worth using.
</Warning>

**The unit is a plug, not a description.** It's what lets Vellum safely connect this factor to a methodology's input in the Workbench — a factor measured *per meal* can fill an input that expects *per meal*, and nothing else. Pick the unit that will match, not the one that reads most nicely.

**Currency belongs in the currency field.** Keep the unit as the denominator only.

<Warning>
  **Check Usage before you deprecate.** If projects are relying on a factor, deprecating it without telling anyone will surface as a surprise at review time.
</Warning>

**Factors can't be bulk-archived.** Unlike the catalogue libraries, factors have no bulk archive — use the status pill on the record instead, so the change is deliberate and one factor at a time.
