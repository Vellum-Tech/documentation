A **beneficiary** is a group that benefits from an activity: students, carers, patients, people experiencing homelessness. It can also be something less obvious — a public system that carries less load, or the environment itself.

The Beneficiary library is your firm's agreed catalogue of those groups. Like the other catalogue libraries it holds phrases, never figures.

## Why it exists

Partly for the same reason as the [Activity library](/product/libraries/activities): consistent wording makes reports comparable, and re-inventing "families and carers" on every engagement is wasted effort.

<Note>
  But beneficiaries carry something the other libraries don't. Some of these groups are people in vulnerable circumstances, and describing them in a public report is not a neutral act. So this library treats consent and privacy as first-class: an entry can be flagged **Sensitive**, and when it is, a safeguard note travels with it everywhere it appears. An analyst about to put that group in a report sees the caveat first, not after publication.
</Note>

## The beneficiary list

Open **Libraries → Beneficiary library**.

Each row shows:

- **Beneficiary** — the group's label, with a sensitivity marker where the group is flagged.
- **Directness** — how directly the group benefits.
- **SDGs** — the goals it's tagged with.
- **Observed** — how often the phrase recurred in the source material. A ranking signal for suggestions only; not a project count, and never part of a calculation.
- **Status** — **Draft**, **Curated** or **Archived**.

Search matches the label. The **Directness** and **Status** filter chips narrow the list, and **View options → Grouping** clusters by either.

### Directness

Four classifications, and they're descriptions rather than judgements:

- **Direct** — the group the activity is aimed at. The young person in the mentoring programme.
- **Indirect** — the group that benefits because the direct group did. Their family, their employer.
- **System** — a service or public system that carries less load. A hospital, a housing waiting list.
- **Environment** — the natural environment as the beneficiary.

Recording this keeps different kinds of claim distinguishable in a report. "We helped 400 young people" and "we relieved pressure on the local health service" are both true and should never be added together as though they were the same thing.

## The beneficiary record

Click a row. The label is the heading, with a status pill beside it and — where the group is flagged — a **Sensitive group** marker.

If the group is sensitive and has a safeguard note, that note is shown in a highlighted panel immediately below the heading, before anything else on the record. It's positioned there on purpose.

**Beneficiary details** holds the **Beneficiary group** label, its **Directness** and its **SDGs**.

**Safeguards** holds the **Sensitive** flag and the **Safeguard / permission note** — the caveat an analyst must see before this group lands in a report.

**Authoring hints** holds **Example activities** (activities that typically serve this group) and **Common outcomes** (changes this group commonly realises). Suggestions offered when someone builds a model; never applied automatically.

**Notes** is free text for context, caveats or wording decisions.

At the foot of the record is a quiet line showing when the entry was added and by whom.

## How to add a beneficiary

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the library and start a new beneficiary">
    Open **Libraries → Beneficiary library** and click **New beneficiary**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the label">
    Type the **Beneficiary group** label — "Families and carers", "People with disability".
  </Step>
  <Step title="File it">
    Optionally set the **Directness** and tag any **SDGs**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the safeguard posture">
    If this is a group requiring care, tick **Sensitive group**. A **Safeguard note** field appears; write the consent or aggregation caveat that anyone using this group needs to see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it">
    Click **Add beneficiary**. Vellum opens the new record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

New entries land as **Draft**.

Note that the sensitivity flag and safeguard note are offered here, at the point of creation, rather than only on the record. The privacy posture should be captured when the group is first described, not retrofitted once someone notices.

## How to edit a beneficiary

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the card">
    Open the beneficiary and find the card you want to change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Edit">
    Click **Edit** on that card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make your changes">
    Make your changes — including ticking or unticking **Sensitive** and updating the **Safeguard / permission note** on the **Safeguards** card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**, or **Cancel** to discard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How to curate a beneficiary

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the entry">
    Open the entry and check the label, directness, SDGs and safeguard posture.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the status pill">
    Click the status pill beside the heading.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Curated">
    Choose **Curated**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How to archive beneficiaries

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tick the rows">
    In the list, tick the rows you want to archive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Archive">
    Click **Archive** on the bar that appears at the bottom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Archived entries stay in the library, marked **Archived**, and can be restored from the record.

## How beneficiaries are used

Each row of a project's impact model pairs an activity, a beneficiary and an outcome. The beneficiary picker offers this library; if someone types a group that isn't in it, they're offered a tick box — *Add this beneficiary to the Beneficiary Library so it can be reused* — ticked by default.

That's how the library grows, and it means new entries arrive as drafts that need occasional tidying.

## Tips and gotchas

<Tip>
  **Write the sensitivity note as an instruction, not a label.** "Consent obtained for aggregate reporting only — do not publish figures below groups of ten" is useful. "Sensitive" on its own tells the reader nothing they can act on.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  **Flag sensitivity when you create the entry.** It's much harder to retrofit once the group is already in three reports.
</Warning>

**Directness stops double-counting.** If a system-level beneficiary and the direct beneficiaries both appear in your model, keeping the classification straight is what stops the same benefit being counted twice.

**The environment can be a beneficiary.** If the change is ecological, say so with **Environment** rather than stretching a human group to cover it.

**Search before adding.** "Carers", "Family carers" and "Families and carers" as three entries is the problem, not the solution.

<Warning>
  **Archive rather than delete.** Published reports referenced these phrases; keep them retrievable.
</Warning>
