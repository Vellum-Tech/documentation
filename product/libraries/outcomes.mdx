An **outcome** is the change an activity creates: improved mental wellbeing, reduced social isolation, avoided landfill, sustained employment. The Outcome library is your firm's agreed catalogue of those phrases.

Outcomes are the smallest of the catalogue libraries by design — a label, some SDG tags and a note. That's all a governed phrase needs to be.

## Why it exists

Outcomes are the hinge of the whole model. A project's impact model is built out of them, and a valuation puts a value against one. If the same change is described three different ways across three projects, you can't compare, you can't aggregate, and your portfolio view is fiction.

Agreeing the wording once fixes that. It also makes valuation easier: when an outcome phrase is stable, the [methodology](/product/libraries/methodologies) your firm uses to value it can be stable too.

<Note>
  Like the other catalogue libraries, an outcome carries no monetary value. It's a phrase. The value comes later, from the engine.
</Note>

## The outcome list

Open **Libraries → Outcome library**.

Each row shows:

- **Outcome** — the phrase itself.
- **SDGs** — the goals it's tagged with.
- **Observed** — how often the phrase recurred in the source material the library was built from. A signal for ranking suggestions; not a project count, and never part of any calculation.
- **Status** — **Draft**, **Curated** or **Archived**.

Search matches the outcome phrase. The **Status** filter chip narrows the list, and **View options → Grouping** lets you cluster by status.

## The outcome record

Click a row. The phrase is the heading with a status pill beside it — click the pill to change status, and it saves immediately.

**Outcome details** holds the **Outcome** phrase and its **SDGs**.

**Notes** is free text: context, caveats, or a note on how the phrase is worded and why. This is the field to use when an outcome sounds simple but isn't — when "improved wellbeing" means something specific to your firm, say so here.

At the foot of the record is a quiet line showing when the outcome was added and by whom.

## How to add an outcome

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the library and start a new outcome">
    Open **Libraries → Outcome library** and click **New outcome**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the phrase">
    Type the **Outcome** phrase. Write it as a change, not an activity: "Improved mental wellbeing", not "Counselling sessions delivered".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag the SDGs">
    Tag any **SDGs** it relates to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add notes">
    Add **Notes** if there's context worth carrying.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it">
    Click **Add outcome**. Vellum opens the new record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

New outcomes land as **Draft**.

## How to edit an outcome

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the card">
    Open the outcome and find the card you want to change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Edit">
    Click **Edit** on that card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make your changes">
    Change the phrase, the SDG tags or the notes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**, or **Cancel** to discard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An outcome always needs a label — clearing it and saving won't work, and Vellum will tell you so and keep the card open.

## How to curate an outcome

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the entry">
    Open the outcome and check the wording and SDG tags.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the status pill">
    Click the status pill beside the heading.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Curated">
    Choose **Curated**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Curated** is your firm saying "this is the phrase we use". **Draft** means it arrived in the flow of work and hasn't been reviewed.

## How to archive outcomes

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tick the rows">
    In the list, tick the rows you want to archive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Archive">
    Click **Archive** on the bar that appears at the bottom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Archived outcomes stay in the library, marked **Archived**, and can be restored from the record.

## How outcomes are used

Every row of a project's impact model names an outcome, alongside an activity and a beneficiary. The outcome picker offers this library, and someone typing a phrase that isn't in it is offered a tick box — *Add this outcome to the Outcome Library so it can be reused* — ticked by default.

Later, in the Workbench, each outcome is valued: a methodology is chosen, its operands are filled from client data or from [factors](/product/libraries/factors), and the engine produces the figure. Where the evidence for an outcome is missing, a [survey template](/product/libraries/surveys) is how you go and get it.

## Tips and gotchas

<Tip>
  **Write a change, not an action.** "Sessions delivered" is an output — it belongs to the activity. "Reduced social isolation" is an outcome. Getting this right is the difference between a report that measures effort and one that measures impact.
</Tip>

**Keep the phrase short and general.** It should read sensibly for any client running a similar programme. Client-specific detail belongs in the project, not in the shared library.

**Use the notes for the definition.** Wellbeing, resilience and inclusion mean different things to different readers. If your firm has a working definition, this is where it lives — and it's what stops the phrase drifting.

**SDG tags are worth the minute they cost.** They're how a reader finds the outcomes relevant to a goal they care about, across every report you produce.

<Warning>
  **Search before adding.** Near-duplicate outcome phrases are the fastest way to make a portfolio view meaningless.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **Archive rather than delete.** Published reports referenced these phrases.
</Warning>
