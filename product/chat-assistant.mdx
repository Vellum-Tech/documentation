## What the assistant is

Vellum has a chat assistant built into every page. It's there to help you find your way around your own work — which organisations you're advising, what's sitting in a project, which piece of evidence is still missing — without you having to remember where you filed something.

Think of it as a knowledgeable colleague who has read everything in your workspace. It can point you at things, explain what it found, and suggest what you might do next. What it can't do is put a number on your report. That's a deliberate design decision, not a limitation we're planning to remove, and it's worth understanding before you use it.

<Warning>
  **A note on where the assistant is up to:** the chat interface is fully built and you can open it, type in it, and move around it today, but it isn't connected to the language model yet. Every reply you get right now is the same placeholder message telling you AI is disabled. This guide describes the interface as it stands and flags clearly what's still coming, so you know what you're looking at when you open it.
</Warning>

## What it can and can't do — the number rule

This is the most important thing to understand about the assistant, so it comes before everything else.

<Note>
  **The assistant will never produce a dollar figure.** Not an estimate, not a rounded guess, not a "roughly". Every monetary value that appears anywhere in Vellum — on a project, in a report, against an outcome — is calculated by the deterministic engine from sourced data and versioned factors. A language model is never anywhere near that calculation.
</Note>

The reason is simple. If a model could nudge a number, even helpfully, then no number in Vellum could be audited. Someone reviewing your report has to be able to trace any figure back through the exact inputs and the exact factor that produced it. A model's output can't be traced that way, so it's fenced out of the arithmetic entirely.

What that means in practice:

**The assistant can:**

- Find things for you — an organisation, a project, an outcome, a document you uploaded months ago
- Explain what a number means, where it came from, and which inputs fed it
- Tell you what's missing — which data requests are outstanding, which outcomes have gaps
- Suggest what to look at next, or which method might suit a situation
- Summarise what's in a record so you don't have to read all of it

**The assistant will not:**

- Calculate or state a dollar value
- Change a figure on a project or a report
- Fill a data gap by estimating what the missing number probably is
- Sign anything off on your behalf

If you ask it what an outcome is worth, the right answer from the assistant is to show you the figure the engine calculated and walk you through how the engine got there — never to work it out itself.

The same rule applies to you, incidentally. Nobody types a number onto a report in Vellum, including you. Numbers arrive from data and factors, or they don't arrive at all.

## Opening and using chat

There are two ways in, and they're the same assistant.

### The chat panel, from any page

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the chat button">
    Look for the round message button in the bottom right corner of the screen. It sits above the page and follows you wherever you go in Vellum.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open the panel">
    Select it. A panel slides in from the right, headed **Vellum Agent**, and the page content shifts left to make room rather than being covered up.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask your question">
    Type your question in the box at the bottom and press Enter, or select the send button.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Resize the panel to suit">
    To make the panel wider or narrower, drag its left edge. It'll go as narrow as a slim column or as wide as about half a large screen, and it remembers the width while you keep working.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Close it when you're done">
    Select the **X** in the top right of the panel to close it. The round button reappears.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The full Chat page

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Chat from the sidebar">
    Select **Chat** in the left sidebar.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Use the same assistant with more room">
    You get the same assistant with a lot more room — a full page, centred, with space for longer answers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask your question">
    Type in the box at the bottom, the same way.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Use the panel when you want to ask something without losing your place. Use the full page when you're settling in for a longer piece of thinking.
</Tip>

### Does it know what I'm looking at?

Partly, and this is quietly one of the most useful things about it.

When you open the chat panel while you're on an [organisation's record](/product/organisations), the panel picks that up. Just under the **Vellum Agent** heading you'll see a small chip with the organisation's name and its logo, followed by the words **· confirmed context only**. The prompt box changes too, from the general **Ask Vellum…** to **Ask Vellum about this organisation**.

That chip is telling you two things. First, the assistant knows which organisation you're asking about, so you can say "what's outstanding here" rather than naming them. Second — and this is what "confirmed context only" means — it will work from what's actually been confirmed and recorded against that organisation, not from general knowledge or inference about them.

When you navigate away from the record, the chip disappears and the assistant goes back to being general-purpose.

Page awareness currently covers organisation records. Awareness of the project, outcome and workbench pages you're viewing is coming.

### Mentioning an organisation

On the full **Chat** page, you can point the assistant at a specific organisation without leaving the conversation. Type an `@` symbol and start typing a name — a small menu appears headed **Organisations**, listing matches from your actual workspace. Use the arrow keys and Enter to pick one, or just select it, and the name drops into your message.

This is handy when you want to compare two organisations, or when you're on the general chat page rather than inside a record. The mention menu is on the full **Chat** page only; the side panel uses the context chip instead.

### Conversation history

Your conversation stays with you while you move around Vellum. Open the panel on one page, ask something, navigate somewhere else, and the thread is still there when you open it again.

<Warning>
  It isn't saved permanently yet, though. Refresh your browser or close the tab and the conversation starts fresh. Saved, named, searchable conversation history is coming — for now, if an answer matters, copy it somewhere before you reload.
</Warning>

### The model selector

At the bottom of the prompt box there's a selector showing **Claude (Demo)**, with **GPT (Demo)** and **Gemini (Demo)** also listed. As the labels suggest, these are placeholders while the assistant is being wired up. Changing the selection doesn't currently change anything about the reply you get. You can safely ignore it.

## Good prompts to try

Vellum offers a few starter prompts as chips just above the box — select one and it drops into your message, ready for you to edit before sending. In the side panel you'll see **Summarise my projects**, **What data is outstanding?** and **Find an outcome**. On the full page they're phrased slightly differently, including **What clients do I have?** and **Find connections across my projects**.

Beyond those, the prompts that suit this assistant best are the ones about finding, explaining and checking:

**Finding your way around**

- "Which projects am I running for this organisation?"
- "Where did we file the annual report they sent through?"
- "Show me the outcomes tagged to community wellbeing."

**Understanding a figure without asking it to produce one**

- "Which inputs feed this outcome's valuation?"
- "What factor is this project using for volunteer hours, and which version?"
- "Explain in plain language what this outcome is measuring."

**Checking what's missing**

- "What data requests are still outstanding on this project?"
- "Which outcomes don't have a source attached yet?"
- "What's blocking this project from being ready for review?"

**Thinking things through**

- "What methods do other projects use for a similar outcome?"
- "Summarise what we know about this organisation's reporting purpose."

Notice the shape of these. You're asking the assistant to locate, explain and check — the things it's genuinely good at and allowed to do. A question like "what's this outcome worth?" is better answered by opening the project, where [the engine's own figure and its full trace](/product/workbench-and-valuations) are waiting for you.

## Tips

**Ask it where something is before you go hunting.** The assistant's real value early on is saving you the click-through. "Where's the engagement letter for this organisation" is a perfectly good question.

**Let the context chip do the work.** If you're already on an organisation's record, don't bother naming them. Say "here" or "this organisation" and the assistant knows who you mean.

**Treat suggestions as suggestions.** When the assistant proposes a method, a proxy or a next step, it's a proposal for you to accept or reject — never something that takes effect on its own. Nothing the assistant says changes your data.

**If an answer involves a number, check it at the source.** The assistant can tell you what a figure is and how it was derived, but the authoritative version always lives on the project itself, with its full trace. For anything going into a report, look at it there.

**Copy anything you want to keep, for now.** Until saved history arrives, a browser refresh clears the conversation.

<Tip>
  **Drag the panel wider for longer answers.** The default width suits quick questions; if you're reading a summary, give it more room.
</Tip>
