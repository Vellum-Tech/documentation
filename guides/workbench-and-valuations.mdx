This is the guide to the part of Vellum where evidence becomes value. Everything here lives on a project's **Quantify** tab, which has three sections in its side rail: **Quantified outputs**, **Workbench** and **Factors**.

## How Vellum turns evidence into value

Think of a valuation as three ingredients coming together.

**A methodology** is a formula. For example, "meals served × cost per meal". The formula has named blanks in it, and Vellum calls those blanks **operands**. Picture each operand as a labelled empty jar: one jar says *meals*, the other says *AUD per meal*.

**A factor** is a sourced reference value from your firm's [Factor library](/libraries/factors), such as the replacement cost of a meal or the value of an hour of volunteer time. Picture a factor as a full jar, already labelled with its unit, carrying a citation and a version number so anyone can check where the number came from.

**A quantified output** is a jar the client filled themselves: their own measured evidence for the reporting period. How many meals they actually served. How many hours were actually volunteered.

Running a valuation is the act of pouring full jars into the matching empty ones. That is what **binding** means. Once every operand has something bound to it, you press a button and the engine does the arithmetic.

### The golden rule

<Note>
**No person ever types a dollar figure onto a report.**
</Note>

You never enter the answer. You choose the formula, and you choose which sourced number goes into each blank. The engine reads each number straight out of its own record (the factor version, or the recorded client value) and computes the result deterministically. It is a calculator, not a judgement call, and it is not an AI. The same inputs always produce the same figure.

This isn't a convention that people are trusted to follow. The engine physically refuses to accept a number handed to it. It only accepts a *pointer* to a record, and then reads the number itself. That is why a valuation figure in Vellum can always be traced: click any dollar amount and you can walk back through the formula, to the operands, to the factor citation or the client's own measured data.

There is a second guard worth knowing about. If an AI assistant has *proposed* a client value, that value cannot be used in a valuation until a named person has confirmed it. An assistant may suggest a number. It may never spend one.

## Quantified outputs

Quantified outputs are the client's own measured evidence for the period: what they did and what they achieved. Meals served. Hours volunteered. Tonnes of food rescued. Never dollars, because these are quantities, and dollars are the engine's job.

If you've come from the old spreadsheets, these are what the "Outcomes (quantified)" column used to hold.

### Recording one

Open the **Quantify** tab, choose **Quantified outputs** in the rail, and select **Record metric**. You'll be asked for:

- **Metric**: what was measured, in plain words ("Food rescued", "Meals served").
- **Value**: the measured quantity as a plain number.
- **Unit**: what the number counts. Vellum suggests units already in use across your firm, so you don't end up with "meal" in one place and "meals" in another. You can still type a new one if you genuinely need it.
- **Period start** and **Period end**: the window this measurement covers. These default to the project's reporting period, which is nearly always what you want.
- **Source**: how you came by the figure, either **Document extraction**, **Client upload**, **Engagement answer** or **Analyst estimate**. This is a claim about evidence quality, so be honest. An analyst estimate is flagged as one everywhere it appears.
- **Source note**: where exactly it comes from. Name the document, the tab, the cell, the conversation. This is what a reviewer or auditor reads first.

Recorded outputs appear in a table showing the metric, its quantity, its period and its source tag. They belong to the organisation, not just this project, so a figure you record once is available to bind wherever it's relevant.

### Editing and re-recording

Recording a metric with a name you've used before creates a new version of it and supersedes the old one. The history isn't destroyed, it's layered. Any valuation already computed against the earlier version keeps pointing at the exact version it used, so an old report never silently changes underneath you. To bring a valuation up to date against a new measurement, you re-run it (see [Reviewing results](#reviewing-results)).

Once a project is locked, this section shows a **Gated** tag and no new evidence can be recorded against it.

## Factors on a project

The **Factors** section is a read-only register. It lists every sourced reference value this project's valuations actually rest on, with the exact version applied.

You don't add factors here. Factors are authored and maintained in your firm's [Factor library](/libraries/factors), and they attach to a project automatically as you bind them to operands in the Workbench. The caption says as much: *Bound in the Workbench, maintained in the Factor library.*

Each row shows the factor's name, its value and unit, the version number applied, its evidence tier (**Published**, **Peer**, **Industry**, **Assumption** or **Firm-internal**) and how many bindings across which activities depend on it.

This section answers one question, and it's the question an auditor asks first: *which rates does this report rest on?* It's also where you'd notice that a single stale rate is quietly holding up six different lines.

## The Workbench

The Workbench is one grid, and that grid *is* the whole valuation. Every valuation line, every data gap and every review note is a row or a column of the same sheet. There are no hidden side panels holding the real numbers.

### The columns

- **#**: the line number.
- **Activity**: the piece of work being valued.
- **Aligned outcome**: the change this line puts a value on. If the line isn't yet linked to an outcome in the project's model, its own label shows here instead.
- **Formula**: a short sentence reading back the actual quantities used, like *50,862 meals × $10.00 AUD/meal*. Click it to open the full evidence trail: every operand, the quantity bound to it, whether it came from a reference factor or client data, and its rationale.
- **Value**: the engine's figure. Click it and you get a small padlock and an explanation rather than a text box. This cell has never taken typed input and never will. To change the figure, you change the evidence behind it and let the engine recompute.
- **Sources**: a compact note of where this line's numbers came from, either *client data*, *reference factor*, or both.
- **Review**: this line's review state, covered below.

Below the rows sits **Total valued impact**, pinned to the bottom of the sheet so it stays in view however far you scroll.

### The toolbar

- **Lock**: freezes the project's calculation permanently (covered under [Sign-off](#sign-off-step-by-step)). Once locked, this becomes a **Locked** badge.
- **Recalculate**: re-reads every line from the engine.
- **Sign-off**: a chip showing where the project sits on the seven-step ladder. Click it to open the ladder itself.
- The **…** menu: holds **New valuation**, **Add gap**, and the exports.

Two exports live in that menu. **Draft report (.docx)** downloads a written draft built from the engine's own figures. **Calculation workbook (.xlsx)** is being moved to the new backend and is temporarily unavailable. It will tell you so if you try it.

### Data gaps

Sometimes an outcome is real but genuinely can't be measured yet, because the survey hasn't run or the baseline doesn't exist. Rather than invent a number or quietly drop the outcome, you record it as a data gap via **Add gap**. You give it an **Outcome**, say **Why it can't be measured yet**, and optionally link an **Activity** and name the **Expected source next round**.

Gaps appear in their own band in the sheet, marked *Data gaps, held at $1 · excluded from totals*. The $1 is a convention meaning "real but unvalued". It is never a stored value and never enters any total. Each gap offers **Request data** (which drafts an evidence request to the client), **Dismiss**, and **Resolve**. Closed gaps stay visible in a quiet trailer band with a **Reopen** action, so a mistaken dismissal is always recoverable.

Open gaps never block sign-off. They're surfaced honestly and excluded by design.

## Running a valuation step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the New valuation sheet">
    Use **New valuation** in the **…** menu, or click the ghost row at the bottom of the sheet that reads *New valuation: the engine computes the figure*. If it's unavailable, it will say why. Either the project is locked, or it has no activities yet (add one on the **Model** tab first).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the Activity">
    This is the piece of work whose impact you're valuing. If the project has exactly one activity, it's chosen for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the Methodology">
    This is the formula. Once selected, Vellum shows the formula in plain form beneath the picker, along with the unit its result comes out in, so you can sanity-check that you've chosen a formula that produces dollars, not hours.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bind every operand">
    The **Operand bindings** section lists one card per blank in the formula, each showing its name and the unit it expects. This is the heart of the work and it has its own section below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a Label and Notes if useful">
    The label is what shows in the sheet and the draft report, something like *Meal relief, market value*. Both are optional.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Compute valuation">
    The button stays disabled until every operand is bound. When you press it, the evidence goes to the engine, the engine reads each bound record, does the arithmetic, and the new line appears in the sheet with its figure.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Re-running a valuation for the same activity and methodology updates that same line rather than creating a duplicate, so its reviews and allocations stay attached to it.

## Binding operands

Each operand card offers three ways to fill the jar. You choose per operand, and you can mix them freely within one valuation. How a number is sourced is a decision you make for this valuation, not a property baked into the methodology.

### Factor library

Choose **Factor library** to bind a sourced reference value. Search the library, pick the factor, and Vellum shows its value, unit and evidence tier. **Change** clears the selection and puts you back into the search.

What actually gets recorded is a pointer to that exact factor *version*. If the library later publishes a new version of the rate, your computed line still shows what it was computed from. Nothing shifts retroactively.

### Client data

Choose **Client data** to bind an already-recorded quantified output. You get a list of the organisation's recorded values, each showing its label, quantity and period. Underneath your choice, Vellum shows its source kind and source note, so you can see the evidence you're leaning on before you commit.

### Enter value

Choose **Enter value** when you have a measurement in hand that isn't recorded yet. You supply the **Value** and a **Rationale & source**, saying where this figure comes from and naming the document, cell or calculation. The unit is fixed by the operand and shown for context.

This mode is not a shortcut past the rules. When you compute, Vellum first *records* what you typed as a quantified output for the organisation, with your rationale as its source note, and then binds that recorded row. Your typed number never reaches the engine as a number. It becomes evidence with a home and an audit trail first, and the engine reads it from there. That's why the project needs a reporting period set before you can use this mode: a recorded measurement has to cover a period.

### Matching the labels: unit mismatches

The jar analogy holds all the way down, because the labels have to match. If an operand expects *AUD per meal* and you bind a factor priced in *AUD per kilogram*, those are different measures and the result would be meaningless.

Vellum checks this at the moment you bind and flags a mismatch inline, in plain words: *Priced in [that unit], but this operand expects [this unit]. The engine binds the number as-is. Check it's the same measure.* You'll see the same warning if a factor carries no unit at all, and the equivalent warning when the unit on a recorded client value doesn't match the operand.

<Warning>
Read that message carefully, because it says exactly what happens: **the warning does not stop you, and the engine does not convert anything.** It binds the number as it stands. There are legitimate reasons to proceed, since a factor may be labelled loosely while measuring exactly the right thing, but proceeding is your professional judgement, and it goes on the record.
</Warning>

### What gets recorded when you proceed

Every binding is captured to the project's source register the moment the valuation computes: which operand, what it resolved to, and the rationale you gave. That register is visible on the **Sources** tab, and it's one of the gates on sign-off, because unreviewed or incomplete entries block the ladder at **Method-approved calculation**. So a binding you pushed through on judgement is not invisible. It's an item somebody has to look at and clear before the report can advance.

If you want to say something more pointed about a particular line, raise a query on it in the **Review** column (below). And where a governance lock is genuinely loosened, such as unlocking a project's reporting methodology after it's been locked, Vellum requires your name and a written reason and records both in the override log alongside a note in the sign-off ledger. Who, why and when are always kept.

## Reviewing results

Every line carries a review state in the **Review** column, and it starts at **Draft**.

Click the chip to open the review dialog. Pick a **Verdict**:

- **Mark reviewed**: you've checked this line, its operands, its evidence and its computed value.
- **Raise a query**: something needs resolving before this line can be trusted. A note is required, saying what.
- **Back to draft**: return a line to unreviewed.

You give **Your name** (required), **Your role** (optional) and a **Note**. Once saved, the chip shows the verdict with your initials and the date. A reviewed line reads *✓ Reviewed · SN · 5 Aug*, and an open query shows as **Open query**.

Two things worth knowing. This is recorded against a named person, never as an automated or AI review. And per-line review is informational: it's how a team tracks its own checking, and it does not itself block the project's sign-off.

To change a figure you've reviewed and found wrong, open the **Formula** cell and choose **Edit operands**, or click the **Value** cell and take the same route. That reopens the valuation with its activity and methodology locked in place, so you're revising the evidence rather than starting over. Press **Recompute** and the engine produces a fresh figure for the same line.

If the methodology has been updated since the line was computed, Vellum tells you so and unlocks the picker, so you can bring the line onto the current version deliberately rather than by accident.

## Sign-off step by step

Sign-off is a seven-step ladder from working draft to auditable package. Every move up or down is typed by a named human and lands in an append-only ledger. There is no auto-advance, and AI is never a final approver.

Open it with the **Sign-off** chip in the Workbench toolbar. The overlay shows the full ladder, whose role owns each step, what's blocking the next move, and the complete history.

The steps, and who owns each:

| Step | Owned by | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft only** | AI / analyst | A working draft. Nothing here is confirmed. |
| **Ready for client query / internal review** | Advisor | Ready to put in front of the client, or for internal review. |
| **Client-confirmed input** | Client data owner | The client confirms the inputs are theirs and correct. |
| **Method-approved calculation** | Methodology lead | The methodology lead has approved the calculation method. |
| **Report-ready draft** | Report writer | The written draft is ready to circulate. |
| **Locked final package** | Authorised approver | The final package is locked; the calculation can no longer change. |
| **Auditable export bundle** | System | The export bundle has been produced. This is mechanical, not an approval. |

### Moving up a step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Sign-off overlay">
    Read where the project currently sits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Advance to [next step]">
    Steps move one at a time. You can't skip.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the dialog">
    It names the role you're approving as, and states plainly that the move is recorded against your name and is not an automated or AI approval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your details">
    **Your name** (required), **Your role** (optional) and a **Note** (optional).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Confirm approval">
    The move lands in the ledger.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If a step is blocked, the button is disabled and the reason is spelled out rather than left to a failed click. There are three gates:

- From **Method-approved calculation** onward, every source register entry must be reviewed and complete.
- From **Method-approved calculation** onward, the project's reporting methodology must be locked, with its version and rationale.
- To reach **Locked final package**, the calculation must already be engine-locked. Use **Lock** in the Workbench toolbar first.

Open data gaps are shown alongside these but never block anything. They're excluded from the totals by design.

### Locking the calculation

<Warning>
**Lock** in the Workbench toolbar is the governance act that freezes the numbers. The dialog is blunt about it: locking confirms gating with the engine and stamps the project as final. The calculation and its figures can no longer change, new valuations are disabled, and evidence can no longer be recorded against the project. It's meant to be permanent.
</Warning>

Because it's a governance act, it records a named human. **Your name** is required, **Your role** is optional, and the dialog notes that the lock is recorded against you, not as an automated act. Press **Lock project** to confirm.

After locking, the whole project record turns read-only. The Workbench, the Model tab, Quantified outputs and the [Data room](/guides/data-room-and-documents) all follow the same lock.

### Stepping back

**Step back** moves the project down one step. A note is always required, because stepping back must say why. Gates don't apply to a retreat. Loosening is always allowed, and it's always attributed.

### What an auditor sees afterwards

Open the **Sign-off** overlay on a completed project and the **History** list shows every move ever made: which step it went from and to, who made it, their role, their note, and when. Nothing is edited or removed, and new entries are only ever appended.

Alongside that ledger, an auditor can follow any figure downward. From a dollar amount in the sheet, the **Formula** cell opens the operand bindings. Each binding names a reference factor version (with its citation and evidence tier, listed in the **Factors** section) or a recorded client value (with its source kind, source note and period). The source register on the **Sources** tab holds one entry per binding. The per-line review history shows who checked what and when.

There is no point in that chain where a number was typed onto the report by hand, and no point where a model produced one.

## FAQ

**Why can't I just type the number in?**

Because a typed number can't be checked. The value of a Vellum report is that every figure traces to a sourced factor or a measured client value, through a published formula, computed the same way every time. If a person could type $412,000 into a cell, none of the rest would be worth anything. The engine is built so that a typed figure isn't merely discouraged, it's impossible to hand one over.

**Then what am I actually deciding?**

Everything that requires judgement: which methodology fits this activity, which factor is the right reference and whether it's credible, whether the client's evidence is sound, whether an outcome should be valued at all or held as a data gap, and whether the whole thing is fit to sign. The arithmetic is the only part taken out of your hands.

**Vellum flagged a unit mismatch. Have I done something wrong?**

Not necessarily, because it's a flag, not a verdict. It's telling you the labels on the two jars don't read the same, and that the engine will use the number exactly as it stands, with no conversion. Check that the factor really measures what the operand asks for. If it does, proceed. The binding and your rationale go to the source register, where someone reviews it before sign-off can advance.

**Can the AI assistant compute a valuation or approve a step?**

No. An [assistant](/guides/chat-assistant) can help you find evidence and can propose client values, but a proposed value cannot enter a valuation until a named person confirms it, and no sign-off step can be taken by anything but a typed human name. AI is never a final approver.

**I need to change a figure that's already been computed. How?**

Open the line's **Formula** or **Value** cell and choose **Edit operands**. Revise the evidence and press **Recompute**, and the same line updates, keeping its reviews and allocations. If the project is locked, you cannot. That's the point of locking.

**What does the $1 next to a data gap mean?**

That the outcome is real but can't be credibly measured yet. It's a placeholder convention, not a stored value, and it's excluded from every total. It sits in the sheet so nobody forgets the outcome exists.

**Why does the Factors section only show some of our library?**

It isn't the library. It's the register of factors this project's valuations actually depend on. Factors appear here as you bind them in the Workbench. The full catalogue lives in the [Factor library](/libraries/factors).

**My valuation won't submit. What's missing?**

The **Compute valuation** button stays disabled until every operand is bound. Check each card: a **Factor library** operand needs a factor selected, a **Client data** operand needs a recorded value chosen, and an **Enter value** operand needs both a value and a rationale. If the whole sheet is unavailable, the project is either locked or has no activities yet.
